Four signals. One glance.

Every Filipino household already reads a numbered, color-coded warning at a glance. Saklolo borrows that grammar for the classifier's urgency: a fixed color, a numeral and a word, always together.

1
Bantay

Watch. Someone is hurt but awake and talking. Contacts are told; no rush.

2
Ingat

Careful. A fall or wound that needs checking. Guide the person present.

3
Mabilis

Fast. Heavy bleeding, head injury, cannot move. Call contact #1 now.

4
Saklolo

Help, now. Unconscious, not breathing, no reply. Everything fires at once.

Meaning is never color alone: numeral, word and filled-bar count carry it too. Red is reserved for level 4 and the SAKLOLO button.

The mark: a shout, rippling out

Saklolo

The red core is the SAKLOLO button, the moment of calling out. Orange, yellow and blue rings travel outward through the signal scale: the alert reaching contacts, then neighbors, then everyone who can help. It is also the sound of a voice carrying across a house.

Rings thin out below 40 pt: the yellow ring drops and strokes thicken (shown at 60 pt and 30 pt). Keep clear space of one ring-width on every side. On light grounds use the deepened ring colors in the lockup. Never rotate, outline, add gradients, or recolor the core.

Night ink, signal color

Emergencies happen in a dark hallway at 2 a.m. and in noon glare on a flooded street. Dark is the default so the screen does not blind; light is first-class for sunlight. Signal colors stay constant in both.

Red means alarm

Only the SAKLOLO button, level 4 and failures use red. Calm actions, like Next, are white on ink. If red is everywhere, nothing is urgent.

Green means done

Green appears only after something actually happened: SMS delivered, contact answered. Never as decoration or as an optimistic default.

One tint

White is the interactive color on ink, ink on paper. Signal colors label urgency; they are never buttons.

How Saklolo talks

Taglish, the way a calm relative would say it: short, one instruction, no jargon, no exclamation points. The phone never matches the user's panic.

One motion: the ripple

When Saklolo is triggered, the rings expand outward from the red core once, in signal order, in about a second (ease-out, no bounce), then settle. While listening they breathe slowly at low opacity. Nothing else animates on its own. With Reduce Motion on, the rings simply appear and hold still.
